Universal Patterns

Themes appearing independently across cultures — the more universal, the harder to explain as coincidence

The Chief Sky Father

Every major pantheon has a supreme male sky deity presiding over other gods. Appears on every inhabited continent.

El Elyon (Hebrew) · Zeus (Greek) · Jupiter (Roman) · Odin (Norse) · Ra/Amun (Egyptian) · Anu (Sumerian) · Izanagi (Japanese) · Brahma (Hindu) · Dagda (Celtic) · Ahura Mazda (Zoroastrian)

The Divine Council

Chief god presides over a council of lesser divine beings who execute cosmic will. Explicitly documented in Psalm 82, Job 1-2, and Enoch.

Bene Elohim (Hebrew) · Olympians (Greek) · Aesir/Vanir (Norse) · Ennead (Egyptian) · Anunnaki (Sumerian) · Devas (Hindu) · Watchers (Enoch)

Dying & Rising God (Debated)

A deity dies and resurrects — most clearly in Osiris, Inanna, and Baldr. The category is genuine but contested — many proposed examples only "die" without confirmed resurrection. Predates Christianity by 2,000+ years in Inanna's case.

Inanna/Ishtar (Sumerian — most confirmed) · Osiris (Egyptian) · Orphic Dionysus (Greek mystery cult only) · Baldr (Norse — post-Ragnarok) · Tammuz/Dumuzi (Sumerian)

The Great Flood

Divine flood destroys humanity; one righteous survivor on a vessel. The Sumerian version (Utnapishtim) predates Noah by ~1,000 years — confirmed by archaeology.

Utnapishtim (Sumerian) · Noah (Hebrew) · Atrahasis (Babylonian) · Deucalion (Greek) · Manu (Hindu) · Bergelmir (Norse)

The Chaos Sea Monster

Sky god defeats a primordial chaos serpent/monster to establish cosmic order. Called "chaoskampf" by scholars. Appears universally.

Leviathan (Hebrew) · Tiamat (Sumerian) · Apep (Egyptian) · Typhon (Greek) · Jormungandr (Norse) · Vritra (Hindu)

The Trickster

A divine figure who operates outside rules, causes chaos, and enables change — not purely good or evil. One of the most universal divine archetypes.

Ha-Satan/Adversary (Hebrew — original role) · Loki (Norse) · Hermes (Greek) · Set (Egyptian) · Susanoo (Japanese) · Coyote (Native American) · Anansi (West African)

Forbidden Knowledge Bringer

A divine/semi-divine figure gives humans knowledge they weren't supposed to have — punished for it. Appears across all traditions.

Azazel (Enoch — weapons/cosmetics) · Prometheus (Greek — fire) · Enki (Sumerian — warned of flood) · Lucifer (Christian) · Quetzalcoatl (Mesoamerican — maize/writing)

The Divine Feminine

A supreme female deity of fertility, war, love, or creation — often paired with or opposed to the sky father. Complex dual nature (love AND war) recurs across cultures.

Asherah (Hebrew, Yahweh's consort in early religion) · Inanna/Ishtar (Sumerian) · Isis (Egyptian) · Hera/Aphrodite (Greek) · Frigg/Freya (Norse) · Amaterasu (Japanese) · Devi/Kali (Hindu)

The Underworld Ruler

A god who rules the realm of the dead — typically not purely evil, just sovereign over death. Christian hell conflates ruler (Hades) with adversary (Satan) — originally separate figures.

Sheol/Ha-Satan (Hebrew) · Hades (Greek) · Hel (Norse) · Osiris/Anubis (Egyptian) · Ereshkigal (Sumerian) · Izanami (Japanese) · Yama (Hindu/Buddhist)

Conquered Gods Become Demons

When one religion defeats another, the defeated religion's gods get demoted to demons or evil figures. Documented across all traditions.

Baal → Beelzebub · Astarte → Astaroth · Asuras (Hindu) were Devas who fell · Persian Daevas → demons · Cernunnos imagery absorbed into Satan iconography
