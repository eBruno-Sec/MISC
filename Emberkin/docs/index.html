Emberkin — Production Design Codex

A family-friendly, fully-3D third-person fantasy adventure and, long-term, a safe game-creation platform. This document is the Phase-0 output: the design canon, technical plan, safety model, and roadmap behind the playable vertical slice.

✓ Implemented in the slice ◇ Designed, not yet built

The browser slice implements the core single-player loop end to end. Everything a browser page cannot honestly be — a server-authoritative, moderated, multiplayer live-service with user-generated content — is fully designed here and clearly marked as not-yet-built. Nothing on this page is presented as shipped that isn't.

01Executive summary

Emberkin is an original third-person action-adventure for children, families, and anyone who likes exploration, puzzles, and cooperative play. A player chooses one of two siblings — Wren or Cael — and a single permanent combat class. An ancient being, Mourne the Stillard, separates the siblings and sets out to freeze the world into changeless "peace." The player searches for the lost sibling, aided by Tainer, a small floating light-being pulled from a lake.

The flagship adventure is designed to double as a showcase for a later safe creation platform, where children can build and publish their own experiences inside strong guardrails. The pillars: approachable for an eight-year-old, meaningful for older players, original, accessible, and safe by design.

02Originality

Emberkin uses broad genre conventions (open exploration, elemental powers, a companion, gliding traversal) but every name, character, creature, region, symbol, and system is original to this project. It does not copy characters, companion designs, dialogue, story, map, creatures, UI, music, or code from any existing product. An originality review runs throughout production: any new proper noun is checked against known franchises before it enters canon.

03Vision & design pillars

  • Easy to understand, hard to master. Clear objectives, friendly onboarding, gradual complexity, optional depth.
  • Wonder over threat. Stakes are emotional (a lost sibling), never graphic. Defeated creatures dissolve into light; nothing bleeds or dies on screen.
  • Curiosity is rewarded. Hidden paths, environmental storytelling, transformation-only routes, vertical traversal.
  • Safe by design. Child safety, privacy, and moderation are foundational systems, not add-ons.
  • Accessible from day one. Reduced motion, high contrast, non-colour-only cues, remap, aim assist, scalable UI — all present in the slice.
  • No predatory monetisation. Cosmetics only; no loot boxes, gambling, pay-to-win, or manipulative urgency.

05Core loop & Game Design Document

Moment-to-moment: explore → discover a landmark or Wardstone → solve an elemental or transformation puzzle → fight or sneak past Stonekin → earn Emberlight → upgrade → unlock new traversal (elements, gliding, Mimic forms) → reach the next story beat.

Session loop: pick up the thread of the search for the lost sibling, attune a new element or master a new Mimic form, clear a region's story arc, and bank progress. Meta loop: the widening map, growing element/transform toolkit, and the mystery of Mourne and the Wellspring.

Controls (slice)

WASD move · Shift sprint · Space jump / glide · Left-click or J attack · E interact · Q or 1–5 element · F Mirrorstep / eject · mouse look · Esc pause. Arrow keys also orbit the camera (no-mouse fallback). Full parity across keyboard/mouse, gamepad, and touch is specified in the accessibility and input sections.

06World guide

The Reach of Aldermere is a bright, broken land held together by the Wellspring — a buried source from which the five elements flow. Long ago the Wellspring cracked; its light scattered into Wardstones across the regions, and stray sparks (Glimmers, like Tainer) drifted loose. The slice takes place in Willowmere Vale: a lake-and-forest valley with a fishing shore, a Stonekin camp in the northern crags, and a windswept ridge where Skywright Pell teaches gliding.

Planned regions (each with original culture, architecture, wildlife, arc)

07Narrative guide

Theme: change is not the enemy of peace — it is the price of being alive. Loss hurts; stillness that prevents loss also prevents growth, joy, and reunion.

Arc: Two siblings set out from Willowmere. Mourne appears and, believing he is being merciful, separates them to "spare" them the pain of the coming stillness. The player wakes seasons later, fishes Tainer from the lake, and follows the Wardstones outward — each restoring a piece of the Wellspring's light and a piece of what happened. The search for the sibling and the fight to keep Aldermere changing are the same journey.

Critical story content is authored and testable. Optional/ambient NPC dialogue may be schedule- and reputation-driven, but never generative without the guardrails in the safety section.

08Character guide

Wren

The sister. Warm, quick, stubbornly loyal. Teal palette. Fully voiced/animated; equal to Cael in every way.

Cael

The brother. Steady, curious, dry humour. Amber palette. Equal to Wren; no gameplay advantage either way.

Tainer

A Glimmer — a small floating spark of the world's first light. Energetic, helpful, brave, funny. Helps without constantly interrupting. Her origin ties to the cracked Wellspring.

Skywright Pell

Old windrider and glide mentor. Runs the Windrider's Trial. Gruff, kind, endless wind metaphors.

Both siblings are equally capable, support every class, have complete animation sets, adapt cinematics/dialogue, and store the choice permanently. Optional renaming can be supported later if voice systems stay natural.

09Villain — Mourne, the Stillard

Motive: Mourne once lost everything to change and grief. He concluded that a world which never changes can never suffer — so he seeks to still Aldermere into a perfect, silent, unchanging calm. He believes this is mercy, which makes him tragic rather than evil.

Method: he cracked the Wellspring to drain the world's "restlessness," scattering the elements. His lieutenants, the Hollow Wardens, spread quiet zones (the Stillfields) where nothing grows or moves. The first Warden the player faces is Hush, a towering construct sealed in a hollow beyond the Stonekin camp — its telegraphed slam and exposed back-crystal teach the boss grammar the later Wardens build on. Connection to the siblings: Mourne separated them not out of cruelty but to "spare" them the pain of the stillness to come. The final confrontation is about proving that change — and the grief that comes with it — is worth keeping.

10Combat

Full-3D, readable, non-graphic. Light/heavy/charged attacks, dodging, telegraphs, stagger, knockback, weak points, and bosses. Elemental attacks change interactions (e.g., Cinder ignites, Rime freezes, Arc powers mechanisms). Feedback uses animation, sound, restrained VFX, impact response, and readable UI — never blood or gore. On defeat, enemies dissolve into ash, mist, or drifting motes of light.

11Classes & weapons

One class is chosen once and is permanent for the save. Players only find weapons of their class; weapons can be equipped, compared, upgraded, or dismantled for Emberlight, with destruction protection (the equipped weapon can't be dismantled; dismantling asks twice). Each class has three tiers in the slice — the starter, a Tier-2 find in the frozen-lake hoard, and a Tier-3 reward from the boss — with power scaling ×1 / ×1.4 / ×1.85 before upgrade levels.

12Progression & token economy

Players earn Emberlight from combat, exploration, and challenges, and spend it to raise weapon level (and, in the full game, character skills, elemental abilities, utility, and cosmetics). The design uses the fewest practical currencies, is data-driven for easy rebalancing, age-appropriate, exploit-resistant, and explicitly non-predatory. In the slice, one currency (Emberlight) upgrades the class weapon.

13Elements & Wardstones

Touching a Wardstone attunes an element permanently; the player switches freely among attuned elements. Each element carries an original name, sigil, and colour — and is never signalled by colour alone (name + sigil always accompany it, for colourblind players).

Implemented puzzle: a Rime ice-wall blocks the path north; equipping Cinder and striking it melts it, opening the route to the Stonekin camp — combining combat, elements, and traversal in one gate.

14Mimic Orb — signature transformation

Certain defeated Stonekin leave a single-use Mimic Orb. Absorbing it performs a Mirrorstep: the player becomes that creature. The player's normal abilities are hidden; only the monster's abilities are shown; the monster form has its own separate health, while the player's real health is preserved exactly. The form ends on manual eject (F) or automatically when monster health reaches zero — and in both cases the player returns with the exact health they held before transforming.

Verified invariantAutomated in-game tests confirm: transforming preserves player HP; manual eject restores it exactly; reaching 0 monster-HP triggers automatic eject and restores the exact pre-transform value. (Tested with HP 63 and 47 — both restored precisely.)

Uses

Infiltration and disguise (guards ignore the Mimic form), reaching creature-only routes, creature-specific abilities, breaking obstacles, and alternate quest paths. In the slice, the player Mirrorsteps into a Grumble to slip through the guarded camp.

15Enemy & NPC AI

Stonekin (Grumble, Boulderback) idle, patrol, notice, chase, and attack, then drift home when they lose the player. Guards use vision/proximity and are fooled by a valid Mimic disguise. Stealth states: Safe → Observed → Suspicious → Detected, surfaced with text + shape, never colour alone, and only shown near a guarded zone. NPCs (e.g., Pell) use fixed roles and authored dialogue. Enemy AI is designed to be fair and learnable.

16Gliding — the Windrider's Trial

The player does not start with a glider. Skywright Pell administers the Windrider's Trial: ride an updraft, then glide through a line of rings before touching down. Passing grants the Windrider's Mark (licence) and a windwing. Gliding is learned traversal, not an element. Upgrades (planned): stamina, air control, turn rate, updraft efficiency, cosmetics.

17Quest framework

Quest types: main, regional, character, exploration, puzzle, hidden, cooperative, boss, transformation, gliding, and challenge. The design avoids repetitive fetch quests, emphasising exploration, mystery, environment, character, elements, transformation, and meaningful choices. The slice threads one continuous questline (fish → attune → melt the gate → earn an orb → infiltrate → learn to glide → free roam).

The following systems are designed, not builtThey belong to the full live-service platform and cannot be honestly implemented inside a single static browser page. They are specified here so the architecture is real and the boundaries are explicit.

18Multiplayer & networking ◇ designed

Solo players can complete the whole story; co-op is additive. The model is server-authoritative: dedicated session servers own movement, combat, damage, enemies, elements, transformations, Mimic Orbs, quests, loot, world state, and gliding. Clients predict and reconcile. A network-facing UI state model (Idle → LocallyPending → ServerConfirmed / ServerRejected → Reconciling → DegradedConnection → Reconnecting → Recovered / Failed) keeps latency legible without blaming the player. Session management, matchmaking, parties, interest management, anti-cheat, reconnection, and version compatibility are all part of the design.

19Creator tools / UGC ✓ local sandbox ◇ publishing

Two toolsets: a professional internal editor and a simplified, child-friendly creator. Creation uses sandboxed, capability-limited tools — never unrestricted code execution. An "Instant Playtest" checkpoints the draft, launches an isolated session with execution/asset limits, and returns the author to their exact editor context. Publishing requires moderation, scanning, review, versioning, rollback, audit logs, and parental permission.

Shipped demonstrator: Emberkin Studio implements the child-friendly creator's core loop locally: grid-snapped placement of seven piece types, erase/undo, a 200-piece asset limit, validated JSON save/load (same defensive rules as game saves), and an instant playtest that spawns you at your start flag and returns you to the exact editor camera, mode, and selection. It is local-only by design — nothing can be published or shared, which is why it needs no moderation pipeline yet.

20Child safety, moderation & parental controls ◇ designed

  • Safe defaults. Child accounts default to non-text communication: symbol/phrase wheels, contextual pings, emotes, cooperative signals. Free-form chat requires eligibility, parental authorization, and server-side filtering (profanity + PII + abuse), with reporting, muting, blocking, and rate limits.
  • Parental dashboard gate. Elevated actions (expanded chat, discoverable profiles, publishing, friend requests, purchases, external links, voice) route through a secure parental-verification flow. A child-solvable arithmetic question is explicitly not an acceptable gate; a PIN alone is not legal consent.
  • Server-side validation. All text, chat, profile, publishing, and creator input is validated on the server; client filters are supplemental only.
  • Data minimisation & limited discoverability, audit logs, escalation, and qualified legal review. The product does not claim any control is impenetrable, and does not claim regulatory compliance without qualified review.

21Security threat model ◇ designed

Threats modelled: account takeover, credential stuffing, session hijacking, injection/XSS/CSRF/SSRF, broken access control & IDOR, replay, item/currency duplication, client-authoritative cheating (movement/damage spoofing), Mimic Orb exploits, malicious uploads, chat abuse, secret exposure, supply-chain compromise, container escape, DoS, and matchmaking abuse. Controls: server authority, short-lived tokens with secure refresh, RBAC, input validation & output encoding, least privilege, secrets management, dependency/container scanning, static/dynamic analysis, CSP for web, and signed builds/assets where justified. Even in the slice, local saves are validated defensively (size limit, safe parse, structural + semantic checks, schema-version rejection, prototype-pollution guard, transactional hydration, no code evaluation).

22Privacy & data ◇ designed

Data minimisation by default; PII protection; parent-owned child data with access, export, and deletion paths; retention limits; regional policy via configuration. Local JSON saves are explicitly non-authoritative — the brief forbids client state governing progression, currency, entitlements, competitive results, moderation, or protected child data, and the slice honours that: its saves are a convenience backup only.

23Engine & architecture (TDD) ◇ full game

Slice engine: the browser vertical slice is built on Three.js (WebGL) with no build step — appropriate for a shareable web demonstration. Full game: a native engine is recommended (Unreal or Unity for 3D quality, animation, and dedicated-server tooling; Godot as an open alternative). The recommendation weighs 3D quality, multiplayer, animation, UGC, licensing, dedicated servers, and containerized server integration. Architecture favours a modular monolith for backend services early, with separately scalable dedicated session servers, and event-driven integration where justified — avoiding premature microservices. Domains: identity, accounts, families, profiles, characters, inventory, progression, quests, world state, sessions, matchmaking, parties, friends, communication, moderation, reports, creator, publishing, assets, economy, entitlements, analytics, notifications, admin, and audit.

24Data & schema ◇ full game

Right store per need: relational DB for authoritative accounts/progression; cache for sessions; object storage for assets; search for published content; event streams for analytics; time-series for metrics. Never make client state authoritative for currency, entitlements, competitive results, protected progression, moderation, or permissions. The slice's save schema (versioned, migratable) is the local-only analogue:

save = { schemaVersion: 1, product: "0.1.0-slice", savedAt: ISODate, id: string, payload: { sibling, klass, hp, maxHp, tokens, weaponLevel, elements{loam,tide,cinder,arc,rime}, activeElement, glideUnlocked, stage, flags{...}, pos{x,z} } } // rejected if schemaVersion > supported, or on structural/semantic failure

25Containers & Kubernetes ◇ full game

Runtime services and dedicated game servers are containerized with multi-stage Dockerfiles, non-root users, health checks, pinned dependencies, scanning, and a one-command Docker Compose local stack (API, auth, game server, DB, cache, broker, object-storage emulator, moderation, analytics, admin, web, reverse proxy, observability). Production targets Kubernetes with deployments/StatefulSets, autoscaling, probes, network policies, rolling updates/rollback, and a game-server orchestrator (e.g., Agones) for session-aware routing. Native engine editors are not forced into containers where that harms workflow.

26CI/CD & observability ◇ full game

CI automates formatting, linting, static analysis, tests, security/dependency scanning, container builds/scans, asset validation, server/client builds, deploy, smoke tests, rollback, and release notes across dev/test/staging/prod with gated releases and branch protection. Observability: structured logs, metrics, tracing, crash reporting, plus game-specific signals (session diagnostics, matchmaking, latency, server tick, moderation turnaround) — never logging secrets, tokens, payment data, or sensitive child data.

27Testing strategy

Full plan spans unit, integration, API, multiplayer, load, security, end-to-end, gameplay automation, save/load, migration, container, deployment, accessibility, moderation, parental-control, exploit, reconnect, and network-degradation tests. In the slice, the acceptance-critical invariants are exercised via an in-game QA harness (enable with ?dev=1): combat → dissolve → orb drop, transform with separate health, manual and automatic eject preserving exact health, element unlock/switch, elemental mechanisms, weapon grant/equip/tier scaling, boss slam cadence and weak-window multiplier, and save serialize/validate/round-trip — including malicious-input rejection and v1 → v2 schema migration. The harness includes a deterministic step(seconds) simulator so time-dependent logic is testable even when the browser throttles animation frames.

28Accessibility

Built in from the start and present in the slice: reduced motion (less shake/flashing), high-contrast theme, larger UI scale, camera-speed and invert-Y, aim assist, and non-colour-only status (every element and alert carries a name/sigil/shape). Targets WCAG 2.2 AA for web interfaces. Planned additions: subtitles with speaker labels, full remapping, hold/toggle alternatives, screen-reader support for menus, and difficulty options. Interactive targets are ≥44px; focus states are visible.

29Art, animation, VFX & audio

Art direction: bright, stylized, readable fantasy with strong silhouettes and expressive faces — clearly 3D, never stick figures. The slice uses a cohesive low-poly, flat-shaded look with PBR-lite materials, soft shadows, and fog. VFX: restrained, readable, non-graphic (dissolve = motes of light). Audio (planned): original adaptive score per region, diegetic ambience, accessible audio cues, and no copyrighted material. An art guide governs proportions, palettes, materials, enemy silhouettes, UI, and asset budgets.

32Release & incident response ◇ full game

Gated, promotable releases (dev → test → staging → prod) with signed artifacts, smoke tests, and rollback. Incident response covers safety escalations (trust & safety on-call), security incidents (containment, disclosure, key rotation), and live-ops outages (runbooks, status, postmortems). Feature flags allow safe, reversible rollout of new regions, creator features, and communication modes.
