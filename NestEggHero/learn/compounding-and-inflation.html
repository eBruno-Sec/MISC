Compounding builds, inflation erodes

6 min | Mathematical education | Reviewed 2026-07-16

What you will learn

  • What compounding means
  • Why today's dollars matter
  • How real return differs from a shortcut

Plain-language summary

compounding makes growth build on itself. inflation reduces purchasing power, so projections should show both nominal dollars and today's dollars.

Growth on growth

The longer the timeline, the more compounding can matter. The rate matters, but time is often the quieter force.

Real return

The exact real-return formula is (1 + nominal return) divided by (1 + inflation), minus 1. Subtracting rates is only an approximation.

Example

$500,000 in 25 years may buy much less than $500,000 buys today if inflation persists.

Risks and exceptions

  • Steady-rate projections hide volatility.
  • Inflation is an assumption.
  • No projection is a promise.

Key takeaways

  • Show assumptions beside projections.
  • Pair nominal dollars with today's dollars.
  • Use exact real-return math when possible.
