Debt payoff: the payment has to beat interest

5 min | Mathematical education | Reviewed 2026-07-16

What you will learn

  • Why too-small payments fail
  • How APR differs from effective annual rate
  • How extra payment affects time

Plain-language summary

Debt compounds too. If the payment does not exceed the interest accrued for the period, the balance does not fall.

Break-even is not progress

A payment that barely covers interest keeps the balance alive. Real progress starts when the payment reaches principal after interest is added.

APR is not always the whole rate

When interest compounds within the year, the effective annual rate can be higher than the stated APR.

Example

On a $6,000 balance at 21% APR, about $105 accrues in the first month. A $100 payment loses ground.

Risks and exceptions

  • Real accounts can include fees.
  • Rates can change.
  • Emergency cash needs matter too.

Key takeaways

  • Interest accrues before the payment in this model.
  • Payments must beat interest.
  • EAR helps compare borrowing costs.
