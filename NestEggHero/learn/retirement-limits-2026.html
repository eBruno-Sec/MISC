The 2026 retirement contribution limits, explained

7 min | Final IRS figures | Reviewed 2026-07-16 | Effective year 2026

What you will learn

  • Which limit belongs to IRAs
  • Which limit belongs to workplace plans
  • Why a headline limit is not a personal recommendation

Plain-language summary

Contribution limits are annual ceilings. For 2026, an IRA limit is $7,500, while the workplace elective-deferral limit is $24,500. Catch-up room can increase those numbers for older savers.

Two families of limits

IRAs and workplace plans are separate families. The 2026 IRA limit is $7,500, plus $1,100 from age 50, for $8,600 when eligible.

Workplace plans such as 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457, and TSP share a 2026 elective-deferral limit of $24,500. The general age-50 catch-up is $8,000, and the age 60 to 63 tier is $11,250 where the plan permits it.

The limit is not a promise

A limit says what the federal rule allows at the top end. It does not decide eligibility, deduction treatment, plan-document access, employer rules, or whether contributing is right for any person.

Example

A 52-year-old in a plan that allows catch-up contributions could have a 2026 headline workplace total of $32,500, made from $24,500 plus $8,000.

Risks and exceptions

  • Limits change by year.
  • Eligibility and deduction rules are separate.
  • Plan documents can be stricter than the headline federal limit.

Key takeaways

  • IRA and workplace limits are separate.
  • Catch-up rules depend on age and plan terms.
  • Every changing number needs an effective year and source.

Sources
