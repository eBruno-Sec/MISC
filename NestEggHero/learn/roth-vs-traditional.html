Roth vs Traditional: tax now or tax later

8 min | General education with IRS figures | Reviewed 2026-07-16 | Effective year 2026

What you will learn

  • How tax timing differs
  • Why future tax rates matter
  • Why neither type is universally best

Plain-language summary

A traditional account can move tax later. A Roth account generally pays tax first and may allow qualified tax-free withdrawals later. The right comparison depends on assumptions and eligibility.

The timing is the heart

Traditional treatment can create a deduction today and tax later. Roth treatment gives up the current deduction and may make qualified withdrawals tax-free later.

One combined IRA limit

For 2026, traditional and Roth IRAs share one combined contribution ceiling of $7,500, plus $1,100 when age 50 or older.

Unknown future rates

The comparison often turns on whether today's tax rate or the withdrawal-year tax rate is higher. Future rates, future law, and personal income are uncertain, so this page teaches tradeoffs rather than choosing for the reader.

Example

If tax rates are identical now and later, Roth and traditional math can tie before other rules. Differences appear when tax rates, RMDs, phaseouts, and eligibility differ.

Risks and exceptions

  • This is not a recommendation.
  • Roth qualified-withdrawal rules have conditions.
  • State taxes are not covered here.

Key takeaways

  • The core distinction is tax timing.
  • IRA contribution limits are shared across traditional and Roth IRAs.
  • Future tax rates are uncertain.

Sources
