IRA phaseouts: the 2026 income tests

7 min | Final IRS figures | Reviewed 2026-07-16 | Effective year 2026

What you will learn

  • What phaseouts do
  • How traditional deduction tests differ from Roth contribution tests
  • Why filing status matters

Plain-language summary

A phaseout is an income range where a tax benefit shrinks. For 2026, traditional IRA deduction and Roth IRA contribution phaseouts use different ranges.

Traditional IRA deduction ranges

For a covered single filer or head of household in 2026, the traditional IRA deduction phases out across $81,000 to $91,000. For married filing jointly with the contributing spouse covered, the range is $129,000 to $149,000.

Roth contribution ranges

For 2026, Roth IRA contributions phase out across $153,000 to $168,000 for single or head-of-household filers and $242,000 to $252,000 for married filing jointly.

Never infer the answer

NestEggHero does not infer filing status, compensation, modified adjusted gross income, or eligibility. It teaches the concept and links to official sources.

Example

A single filer covered by a workplace plan may see a traditional IRA deduction phase out across $81,000 to $91,000, while Roth contribution eligibility uses $153,000 to $168,000.

Risks and exceptions

  • MAGI is not the same as salary.
  • Filing status changes the range.
  • State tax rules are outside this lesson.

Key takeaways

  • Phaseouts are ranges, not cliffs for every rule.
  • Traditional deduction and Roth contribution tests differ.
  • Never guess eligibility from one number alone.
