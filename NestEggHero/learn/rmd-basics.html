Required minimum distributions without panic

6 min | IRS educational summary | Reviewed 2026-07-16 | Effective year 2026

What you will learn

  • Which accounts generally require lifetime RMDs
  • How Roth treatment differs
  • Why aggregation rules matter

Plain-language summary

Traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRAs and most covered plans generally require required minimum distributions beginning at age 73. Roth IRAs and designated Roth accounts do not require lifetime RMDs from the original owner.

The starting age

The general beginning age is age 73, but plan documents, employment status, ownership status, and beneficiary rules can change details.

Aggregation is not universal

IRA RMDs are calculated separately but can generally be withdrawn in aggregate from one or more IRAs. 401(k) and 457(b) RMDs generally cannot be aggregated across plans.

Example

A retiree with three traditional IRAs may calculate each amount and take the combined total from one IRA. Two old 401(k)s generally need separate withdrawals.

Risks and exceptions

  • Beneficiary rules are different.
  • Plan terms can vary.
  • Missing an RMD can have tax consequences.

Key takeaways

  • RMDs are account-specific.
  • Roth lifetime rules differ from traditional account rules.
  • Aggregation is limited.
