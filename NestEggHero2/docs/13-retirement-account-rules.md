# Retirement Account Rules

**Last fact-check:** 2026-07-16  
**Scope:** U.S. federal educational summary. Plan documents and individual facts can change outcomes.

## Verified 2026 distinctions

- IRA contribution limit: $7,500, plus a $1,100 age-50+ catch-up.
- 401(k), 403(b), governmental-457 elective-deferral limit: $24,500.
- General age-50+ catch-up: $8,000.
- Higher age 60–63 catch-up: $11,250 where applicable.
- Defined-contribution annual-additions limit: $72,000, excluding catch-up contributions.
- SEP maximum: generally the lesser of the applicable compensation-based limit or $72,000.
- General SIMPLE employee limit: $17,000, with specialized SECURE 2.0 rules for certain plans.
- Contribution limits, deduction limits, and Roth eligibility phaseouts are separate concepts.

## Required minimum distributions

- Traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRAs and most covered retirement plans generally require RMDs beginning at age 73.
- A workplace-plan participant may sometimes delay RMDs until retirement, but not if the participant is a 5% owner.
- Roth IRAs and designated Roth accounts do not require lifetime RMDs from the original owner; beneficiary rules still apply.
- IRA RMDs are calculated separately but can generally be withdrawn in aggregate from one or more IRAs.
- 401(k) and 457(b) RMDs generally cannot be aggregated across plans.
